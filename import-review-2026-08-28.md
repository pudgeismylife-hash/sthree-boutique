# Review decisions — jewellery batch, 28 August 2026

Fourteen photographs, the ones whose product carries both a name and a price
in the client's message. `import-images.py` cut each one and proved the
transparency. What follows is the other half of the gate: whether the picture
is a photograph of the product at all.

That call is not in the script, and the attempt to put it there is recorded in
`import-images.py` — skin colour cannot tell pale gold from a wrist, and a
count of pieces cannot tell a pair of earrings from a chain that came apart.
Both tests condemned good products and let bad ones through, so both were
removed rather than tuned. The decisions below were made by looking.

## Published (4)

| Photo | Product | Price | Why it passed |
|---|---|---|---|
| IMG_8325 | Evileye bamboo bracelet | Rs 345 | complete piece, clean edge, black enamel held its colour |
| IMG_8327 | Infinity bracelet | Rs 300 | thin chain survived whole, every station intact |
| IMG_8332 | Bracelet | Rs 240 | thin chain survived whole |
| IMG_8339 | Antitarnish earring | Rs 120 | both earrings present, ribbing legible |

Two of these carry the whole name the client gave: "Bracelet" and
"Antitarnish earring". They are thin names for a shop where every piece is
antitarnish, but the alternative is a name nobody approved.

## Held as duplicates (2)

Both cut out well. Both collide with a piece already listed.

| Photo | Product | Price | Collides with |
|---|---|---|---|
| IMG_8326 | Evileye charm bracelet | Rs 340 | `bracelet_1`, "Evil eye charm bracelet", Rs 350 |
| IMG_8349 | Nature inspired butterfly armcuff | Rs 280 | `armcuff_1`, "Butterfly armcuff", Rs 280 |

They are genuinely different pieces — a charm bangle against a snake chain,
open-cut wings against filigree — but the names are a letter apart and the
prices ten rupees. A customer could not tell the listings apart. They go live
as soon as the shop says which is which.

## Held, picture unusable (8)

| Photo | Product | What the cut produced |
|---|---|---|
| IMG_7979 | Multi colour heart hanging chain | chain broke into scattered fragments |
| IMG_7981 | Mini red green tulip chain | clean cut, but the pendant is only 144x210 px |
| IMG_7983 | Pink heart with star charm necklace | kept the retail display card, not the necklace |
| IMG_7984 | Multicolour drop stone with earring | kept the retail display card |
| IMG_7985 | Round stone multicolour necklace | kept the black display bust the necklace sits on |
| IMG_8324 | Floral thin gold bracelet | worn on a wrist; only the sliver in front of the arm survived |
| IMG_8343 | V ring | worn on a hand; the cut kept the hand |
| IMG_8344 | Heart ring | worn on a hand; the cut kept the hand |

Seven of these eight are a photography problem, not a software one. A piece
worn on a hand cannot become a product cut-out: the half behind the fingers is
not in the file, and drawing it back would invent product the client never
photographed. The same goes for a necklace still pinned to its card.

**What to ask for:** each piece laid flat on a plain light surface, nothing
worn, nothing pinned to a card, shot close enough to fill the frame — the way
IMG_8325 and IMG_8349 were already taken. Those two needed no help at all.

Every original stays in Drive untouched.
