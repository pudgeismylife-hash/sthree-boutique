### 3. Every product is now framed by the piece, not by the file

This is the substantial change, and it is a rule rather than a one-off fix.

**The symptom.** The bow anklet's card cut the chain off both ends. The vanilla
satchel floated in a third of its card. Bags and earrings across the shop sat
small in the middle of their own cream.

**The cause.** The build already had a "trim to the picture" step. It was
measuring the file rather than the piece, for two separate reasons:

- Background removal leaves a **haze of alpha 1 to 3 out to the file edges**, and
  `Image.getbbox()` counts any pixel above zero. Its box was therefore the whole
  file every time and the trim did nothing. The vanilla satchel is 667px wide
  inside a 1024px file and was being framed as though it were 1024.
- Several cut-outs carry a **stray hairline pinned against one edge** — a column
  a pixel or two wide left behind by the removal. That held the box open just as
  effectively. On the petal drop earrings it was visible on the card as a scratch
  down the right-hand side.

**The fix.** The piece is measured by its parts, not by its extremes. Connected
components are labelled; anything holding less than half a percent of the picture
is not the piece. Across this catalogue the largest stray holds 0.15% and the
smallest real part — one earring of a pair — holds 45%, so nothing sits near the
line. If that ever stops being true and the small parts add up to real cloth, the
trim refuses itself and the whole file is used.

**The framing rule.** One rule, with the picture deciding which half applies:

| | treatment | why |
|---|---|---|
| **Cut-out** — the piece isolated on transparency | fitted whole inside the frame, undistorted, one consistent margin | cropping a cut-out cuts the piece |
| **Photograph** — worn on the arm, shot close on fabric | fills the frame, centred | what a crop takes off it is backdrop |

The two are told apart automatically by how opaque the picture is out to its own
corners: at or above 85% it is a photograph. That separates this catalogue
cleanly with no borderline case — the nearest are 88.8% and 70.8%.

The same rule now frames the six category tiles. The hand-added insets that were
shrinking bags and nail sets (10%/14% on cards, 14%/18% on tiles) are gone.

**Result**, across 53 catalogue images:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Average fill of the card | 57% | **66%** |
| Bags | 23–34% | **76–79%** |
| Earrings | 38–53% | **70–88%** |
| Tall clothing | 51% | 50% — unchanged bar the margin |

Two cards deliberately got **smaller**: the satchel's second view (98% → 76%) and
the bow anklet (74% → 60%). Both were filling the frame by cutting into the
piece. Smaller and whole is the intended trade.

**No source photograph was altered, regenerated or retouched.** Only the framing
of the thumbnails built from them changed. The originals in `assets/products/`
are byte-identical.

Constants live at the top of `build-og-images.py` and are mirrored in
`build-tiles.py`:

```
FAINT = 4        # alpha below this is invisible haze, not the piece
SPECK = 0.005    # a part holding less of the picture than this is not the piece
KEEP  = 0.98     # ... and the trim is refused outright if it would cost this much
SOLID = 0.85     # opaque this far out to its own edges and it is a photograph
INSET = 4        # the one margin, in pixels of the 360x450 card (8 on the tile)
```

## New traps, added to the list

- **`Image.getbbox()` on an alpha channel trims nothing.** These cut-outs carry a
  haze of alpha 1–3 to the file edges, so its box is the whole file. Use
  `alpha_box()`, which thresholds and then measures by connected component.
- **Cut-outs carry stray hairlines at their edges.** A 1px column left by
  background removal will hold the bounding box open and show on the card as a
  scratch. The component test drops it; a plain threshold does not.
- **A wait-loop whose command string names the process it waits for matches
  itself** and never exits. `until ! pgrep -f "build-og-images.py"` blocked for
  ten minutes today after the build had already finished. Match on something the
  waiter does not contain, or check the output file.

The stale-checkout rule and `./check-sync.sh` are unchanged and still the first
command of every session.
